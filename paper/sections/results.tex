The study population included 67,845 patients with stroke registered in the
Norwegian Stroke Registry between 2014 and 2023, of whom 12,612 \((18.6\%\))
had uncertain-onset stroke (table \ref{tab:baseline}). Most patient
characteristics were similar between patients with uncertain-onset stroke and
all other strokes, with standardized mean differences generally below 0.10. The
largest differences were observed for living situation, and thrombolysis, while
differences in demographic characteristics, stroke type, and cardiovascular
history were modest. Living alone was more common among patients with uncertain-onset
 stroke (45.2\% vs 37.2\%), and they recieved thrombolysis less frequently (1.3\% vs 22.0\%).

\subsection*{Temporal and geographic variation in uncertain-onset stroke}

The crude proportion of uncertain-onset stroke varied over time, with the heigest proportion in 2014
(27.9\%) and the lowest in 2020 (14.0\%). The proportion declined during the early
years of the study period, with a marked reduciton between 2017 and 2018.
 It remained relatively low through 2020, followed by a gradual increase to 
 18.5\% in 2023 (figure \ref{fig:desc_temp_geo_var}A). 


\begin{figure}[ht]
  \caption{Temporal and geographic variation in uncertain-onset stroke. A) Annual proportion of patients with uncertain-onset stroke, 2014–2023. B) Municipality-level geographic variation in uncertain-onset stroke.}
  \label{fig:desc_temp_geo_var}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/fig_2.png}
\end{figure}

In addition to the temporal variation, substantial geographic variation in
 uncertain-onset stroke was observed across municipalities, ranging from 0\% to 62\%  with a median of 16.7\% (IQR 10.2\% and 25.8\%) (Figure \ref{fig:desc_temp_geo_var}B). 
 Areas with relatively high and low proportions appeared to cluster together geographically
 rather than an even distribution across the country.
